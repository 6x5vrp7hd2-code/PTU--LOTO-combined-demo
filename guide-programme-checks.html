07 / CONFLICT CHECKS

Programme checks

The application compares the equipment and times against the published programme, and flags anything that may clash.

When the check runs

Twice: when a request is reviewed, and again when the safety document is issued. The second check matters because the plan may have changed and the locks chosen may differ.

What it compares

The equipment and time window of the work against each restriction on the published programme. A restriction runs from the clearance deadline, or from the scheduled time if no deadline is set, until the restricted-until time.

The four results

ResultMeaningWhat you do
Same equipmentThe work and the activity are on the same item.Blocked. It must be resolved.
Directly connectedThe items are one connection apart, such as a switch unit and its transformer.Blocked. It must be resolved.
Same networkThe items share a ring or feeder group, but are not next to each other.Review it and confirm you have done so.
No conflict recordedNothing is recorded for this equipment and time.Carry on with the normal checks.
Why connected equipment matters:

An activity and a request can use different asset names yet still clash. A permit on a switch unit and an energisation of the transformer it feeds are recorded against different items, so matching names alone would miss it.

When a conflict is blocked

What an AP or SAP can do

Defer the request, ask the contractor to change the time or scope, or ask the Lead SAP or Control Engineer to resolve it.

What the Lead SAP or Control Engineer can do

Record a resolution with their name and a reason. It applies only to that programme revision. If the programme is republished, the conflict shows again and needs a fresh decision.

SituationAt request reviewAt document issue
Programme not published for that dateShows as not checked. The AP or SAP confirms it will be checked again.Today’s programme must be published, or a resolution recorded.

A resolution does not authorise anything.

It records a planning decision. It does not authorise switching or work, and it does not replace the Control Engineer’s authority or the approved switching process.

The check is only as good as the information behind it.

A clear result means nothing is recorded. It does not show that equipment is safe or available. The connection map must match the approved drawings, and LV connections still need to be mapped.

06 / BEFORE ANY DOCUMENT

Document requests

No safety document is considered until a contractor’s request has reached the AP office and been reviewed.

  1. Contractor submits
  2. AP/SAP reviews
  3. Programme check
  4. Decision
  5. Isolation planning
  6. Safety document

What the contractor provides

Who

Contractor, requesting person, contact details and the proposed recipient (CP).

What

The document needed (HV PTW, LV PTW, SFT or LOA), the work location, a description of the work and the RAMS reference.

Which equipment

Chosen from a list, not typed. Free text could hide a conflict because two names might describe the same asset.

When

The date and time the document is required from and until.

Each request receives a unique reference.

For example, DR-2026-0048. The issued safety document keeps this reference, so every document traces back to its request.

What the AP or SAP checks

  • The proposed work activity is sufficiently described.
  • The RAMS and supporting information are suitable.
  • The equipment is correctly identified.
  • The proposed recipient is appropriately authorised.
  • The requested isolation does not conflict with existing documents.
  • The programme check has been reviewed.
  • The work can be accommodated within the operational programme.

The four outcomes

DecisionWhat happens next
Accept for planningThe request moves on to isolation planning.
Request informationIt returns to the contractor with a comment. Their reply brings it back to the AP office.
DeferIt is held because of an operational or programme constraint, and can be reopened later.
DeclineIt is closed with a recorded reason. The contractor can submit a new request.
Accepted does not mean permitted.

Accepted for planning means enough information has been provided to start planning. It is not permission to work, and it is not authority to issue a safety document.
